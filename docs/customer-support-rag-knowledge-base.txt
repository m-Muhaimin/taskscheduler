Customer Support RAG Knowledge Base — TaskScheduler AI Platform> Concise, plain-language reference for customer support agents. Covers what customers experience, how to answer common questions, when to escalate, and how to look up what happened. Technical depth is intentionally low — this is for support, not engineering.---1. What TaskScheduler IsTaskScheduler is an automated SMS assistant that tradespeople (plumbers, electricians, HVAC, handymen, locksmiths) connect to their Twilio phone number and Google Calendar. It handles three jobs:Booking confirmations — when a job is added to the tradesperson's calendar, the system texts the customer the details and a way to confirm or reschedule.Reschedule requests — when a customer replies to reschedule, the AI checks the calendar and offers 2-3 alternative times to pick from.Missed call recovery — when a call is missed, the system sends an auto-text within 30 seconds offering help, turning missed calls into conversations.The tradesperson is the business owner. TaskScheduler is the tool they use — it is not itself a business that customers have a relationship with.---2. What Customers Experience2.1 Booking confirmation SMSExample:> "Hi [Name]! Your [service] with [Tradesperson] is booked for [date] at [time]. Reply CONFIRM to lock it in, or text RESCHEDULE if you need to change the time. Questions? Just reply."Customer can reply:Confirm — "CONFIRM", "yes", "confirmed", "book it", "sounds good", "perfect", "count me in", etc.Reschedule — "RESCHEDULE", "r", "move", "change my appointment", "change my time", etc.Question or new inquiry — "do you do water heaters?", "I need my AC fixed", "what are your hours?" — the AI handles or escalates.2.2 Reschedule flowCustomer replies to reschedule.AI checks the tradesperson's calendar.AI texts 2-3 numbered alternative slots.Customer replies with a number (e.g. "2" or "option 2").AI confirms the new time and updates the calendar.If no slots are available, the AI suggests alternatives and may escalate to the tradesperson.2.3 Missed call auto-textCall is missed (after ~3 rings).Within 30 seconds, customer gets: "Sorry I missed your call! I'm [Name], a [trade]. Text your service need and I'll reply ASAP."If the customer replies with a service request, it becomes a new lead and a booking conversation starts.If no reply within 1 hour, no further messages are sent.2.4 What the AI can handleNew booking requests ("I need my AC fixed", "can you send someone to check my heater")Reschedule ("can we move my appointment", "change my time", "reschedule", "r")Cancel ("cancel my booking", "I need to cancel")Questions ("do you do water heaters?", "what are your hours?")Emergencies — flagged as high urgency ("my heater is leaking RIGHT NOW", "water is flooding")Help ("help", "what are my options", "what can I do")If the AI is unsure (confidence below a threshold), it escalates to the tradesperson and may send a clarifying SMS.---3. Common Questions & Answers"Why did I get this text?"The tradesperson you're dealing with uses TaskScheduler to automate booking confirmations and rescheduling. You got this because they have your number on file from a booking or inquiry. Just reply to the message like a normal text."How do I confirm my booking?"Reply "CONFIRM", "yes", "confirmed", "book it", "sounds good", or similar. You'll get a confirmation SMS and the booking is locked in."How do I reschedule?"Reply "RESCHEDULE", "r", "move", "change my appointment", or similar. The AI will text you 2-3 options. Reply with the number of the slot you want."Can I cancel?"Reply "CANCEL", "cancel my booking", or similar. The system will process the cancellation. Note: depending on the tradesperson's policy and whether a deposit was paid, cancellation may have financial implications — check your booking confirmation for details."I got a text but I never booked anything."Possible reasons: the tradesperson has your number from a previous inquiry or missed call and sent an auto-text; you booked through another channel (phone, website) and the system picked it up; or it's a mistaken number. Reply "HELP" or explain the situation — the AI or tradesperson will sort it out. If it's the wrong number, the tradesperson can remove your number."I replied but got no response."Possible reasons: the message was ambiguous and got escalated to the tradesperson who hasn't responded yet; the AI didn't understand and is waiting for clarification; or the tradesperson is on a job and can't respond immediately. Try replying again with a clearer message. If there's still no response after a few hours, contact the tradesperson directly."The offered time doesn't work for me."Reply with the slot number that works, or reply "RESCHEDULE" and explain your preferred day/time. The AI will try to find a slot that fits. If none are available, the tradesperson may need to manually find a time."I have a billing/payment question."TaskScheduler handles booking and scheduling, not payment disputes. For deposit or invoice questions, contact the tradesperson directly. Deposits are processed via Paddle — the tradesperson manages their own payment policies."I'm getting multiple texts / spam."The system limits missed call auto-texts to 2 per number per day, and stops after 3 missed calls without a reply. Reminder SMS are sent at most once. If you're still receiving unwanted messages, reply "STOP" (if the tradesperson has that configured) or contact the tradesperson to remove your number. TaskScheduler sends only booking-related transactional SMS, not promotional messages."I need service urgently / it's an emergency."If your message indicates urgency (e.g. "leaking RIGHT NOW", "flooding"), the AI flags it as high urgency. But TaskScheduler is a scheduling assistant, not an emergency dispatch service. For true emergencies (gas leaks, flooding, electrical hazards), call emergency services or an emergency hotline. The tradesperson may be able to offer an urgent slot if available.---4. What NOT to Tell CustomersDon't explain technical details about the AI, Twilio, or the platform. Keep it simple: "It's an automated SMS assistant that helps with booking and rescheduling."Don't promise response times, specific slot availability, or that the AI will definitely understand their message.Don't handle payment disputes, refunds, contract terms, or privacy complaints — escalate to the tradesperson.Don't guess. If you don't know why the customer got a specific text, check the conversation log or escalate to the tradesperson.Don't access more customer data than needed to answer the question.---5. When to Escalate to the TradespersonEscalate when:Customer is unhappy or angry — the AI can't resolve negative emotions.Billing/payment dispute — refunds, deposits, invoices. The tradesperson owns this.Ambiguous message the AI couldn't classify — the AI has already escalated internally; the customer may be waiting.Wrong number / mistaken contact — the tradesperson needs to remove the number.Customer wants to stop receiving messages — the tradesperson needs to configure STOP handling or remove the number.Emergency situation — the AI flags urgency but doesn't dispatch. For true emergencies, the customer should call emergency services. The tradesperson decides if they offer urgent slots.Technical issue ("not getting your texts", "texts go to spam") — may be Twilio delivery or phone settings. Escalate to the tradesperson.Repeated messages with no response — the customer may have been waiting too long. The tradesperson should respond manually.---6. How to Look Up What HappenedIdentify the customer — ask for the phone number they used for the SMS and the tradesperson's name/business.Look up the conversation — in the TaskScheduler dashboard, find the tradesperson's account, then look at their SMS history / conversations. Every inbound and outbound SMS is logged with timestamps, direction, content, and AI intent.Check the booking — if it was about a booking, look it up in the calendar. Check status (pending/confirmed/rescheduled/completed/cancelled), date/time, and SMS history.Check escalations — if the AI escalated something, there will be an escalation record with the reason.Check missed calls — if the conversation started from a missed call, there will be a lead record with the first contact timestamp and any SMS replies.---7. Common Issues & FixesIssue	Likely Cause	What to DoDidn't receive booking confirmation SMS	Twilio delivery failure, phone blocking the number, spam filter	Check SMS delivery status in the system. If failed, tradesperson can resend or check Twilio settings. Ask customer to check spam folder and allowlist the number.AI "didn't understand" the message	Message was ambiguous, or AI confidence was below threshold	Check conversation log — the AI may have escalated. Follow up manually as the tradesperson.Reschedule offer had the wrong time	AI interpreted the date incorrectly (e.g. "next Friday" vs "this Friday")	Check conversation log for what the AI parsed. Confirm the correct date with the customer and reschedule manually if needed. The AI shows the parsed date in the confirmation SMS for the customer to correct.Got texts after cancelling	Cancellation may not have been processed, or system sent a follow-up	Check booking status. If cancelled, check why follow-ups were sent. Tradesperson may need to adjust SMS templates or manually stop further messages.System says "no availability" but tradesperson has time	Calendar is full, or AI couldn't find a matching slot	Check the calendar manually. Tradesperson may need to make time or offer a different date.Missed call auto-text wasn't sent	Twilio webhook may have failed, or call didn't register as missed	Check the missed call log. If webhook failed, tradesperson may need to check Twilio configuration.---8. Privacy & DataTaskScheduler stores the customer's phone number, name (if provided), and SMS conversation history with the tradesperson's account.SMS conversations are logged for audit and dispute resolution.The customer's data is owned by the tradesperson who collected it. TaskScheduler doesn't use it for anything other than the booking/scheduling flow.To remove data, the customer contacts the tradesperson — the tradesperson deletes the number from their system. TaskScheduler doesn't have a separate customer database.For privacy complaints or GDPR/deletion requests, escalate to the tradesperson.---9. Key Facts for Support AgentsTaskScheduler is a tool, not a business. The tradesperson is the business owner.The AI is not perfect — it handles common cases well but escalates ambiguous ones. "The AI got it wrong" is expected for edge cases. The fix is manual follow-up by the tradesperson.SMS credits/cost — the tradesperson pays for SMS via Twilio. TaskScheduler doesn't charge the customer. Standard SMS rates from the customer's carrier may apply.Deposits via Paddle — some bookings may have a deposit. The deposit is handled by Paddle, not TaskScheduler directly. Payment issues → contact Paddle or the tradesperson.Timezone — the tradesperson sets their business hours and timezone. The AI uses the tradesperson's timezone for availability. If the customer is in a different timezone, the offered times are in the tradesperson's timezone — the confirmation SMS should make this clear.---10. Escalation ContactsSituation	Who to ContactTechnical issue with Twilio/SMS delivery	Tradesperson (manages Twilio account) + TaskScheduler tech support if platform bugPayment/deposit issue (Paddle)	Customer contacts Paddle support or the tradespersonPrivacy/data deletion request	Tradesperson (owns customer data)AI behavior issue (consistently wrong classifications)	TaskScheduler tech support — log conversation + AI classification for reviewPlatform outage or bug	TaskScheduler tech supportCustomer wants to stop using the service	Tradesperson — remove customer's number or pause auto-SMS---End of document.
